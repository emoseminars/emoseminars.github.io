# Keep every line of the abstract indented by two spaces.
abstract: |-
  Power system operation has traditionally relied on a preventive paradigm, in which operating points are secured in advance against a predefined set of contingencies or uncertainty realizations. The growing share of energy-limited resources, such as renewables and batteries, challenges this paradigm. For instance, a profit-seeking battery may already admit a trajectory from which it can no longer supply energy during critical hours later in the horizon. To prevent such occurrences, existing administrative mechanisms, such as minimum state-of-charge requirements, extend preventive logic to storage but operate outside the market and do not reconcile private arbitrage objectives with system reliability. We propose to use reachability analysis as a methodological enabler of the transition from preventive to adaptive security. Rather than enforcing fixed or a priori reserve and security margins, the proposed approach dynamically identifies the point of no return, defined as the latest time at which a storage unit can change its trajectory and still reach a secure state-of-charge band with a prescribed probability. A stopping-time reward is introduced to price this decision, thereby aligning private battery incentives with system risk. Results based on six years of PJM market data demonstrate target coverage, stable stopping times, and low profit variability. A case study on data center integration further shows that reachability improves the performance of both data centers and the power system.

bio: |-
  Dr. Yury Dvorkin, Ph.D., is an Associate Professor at Johns Hopkins University, with appointments in Civil and Systems Engineering, Electrical and Computer Engineering, and the Ralph O'Connor Sustainable Energy Institute (ROSEI). He serves on ROSEI's Leadership Council and is the U.S. Director of the NSF Global Climate Center on Electric Power Innovation for a Carbon-Free Society (EPICS). He is also a member of ARROW, the Department of Energy's Academic Center for Reliability and Resilience of Offshore Wind. Dvorkin is trusted to serve as an advisor and/or a board member in various government, inter-government, NGO and philanthropic organizations. 
  
  Dr. Dvorkin develops mathematical models and computational algorithms that help decision-makers integrate the technologies reshaping the grid - intermittent generation, energy storage, and data centers - while balancing tradeoffs across cost, reliability, environmental impact, and public health. He also works to translate these modeling insights into actionable policy recommendations for regulators, system operators, and lawmakers. Thus, most recently in March 2026, Dvorkin’s group published the [report](https://energyinstitute.jhu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Data_Center_Analysis_PJM-March-20-2026.pdf) on data center flexibility in PJM found that a single change to how data centers connect to the grid would save electricity customers $15–16 billion annually and improve grid reliability as new generation and transmission capacity comes online.
  
  His research has been supported by NSF, DOE, DOD, ARPA-E, State of Maryland, State of New York, Commonwealth of Massachusetts, Alfred P. Sloan Foundation, Blue Horizon, Energy Innovation, EPRI, and industry partners including ISO NE, Meta, Constellation and Amazon.
  
  Dr. Dvorkin is the recipient of the 2016 Scientific Achievement Award from the Clean Energy Institute at the University of Washington, an NSF CAREER Award (2019), a Goddard Junior Faculty Fellowship (2019), a JHU Discovery Award (2023), an IEEE Outstanding Associate Editor Award (2025), a JHU Provost's Fellowship for Public Engagement (2025), and the Award for Distinguished Service from JHU's ROSEI (2026). He also holds numerous additional research and leadership roles and serves on/leads multiple IEEE, ACM, INFORMS and NAE working groups and committees, including the organizing committee committee of the inaugural [Power Up conference](https://power-up.us) where he serves as the TPC Chair (joint with Dr. Line Roald).

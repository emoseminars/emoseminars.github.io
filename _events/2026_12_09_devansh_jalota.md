# Keep every line of the abstract indented by two spaces.
abstract: |-
  The growing integration of renewables and distributed energy resources is transforming electricity markets, introducing greater uncertainty and new challenges for reliable and efficient operation. As electricity markets become increasingly decentralized, market operators must coordinate the decisions of diverse energy suppliers despite limited information and growing uncertainty about their incentives and responses to prices. Yet traditional equilibrium pricing approaches rely on precisely this information, motivating the need for market mechanisms that can learn from observed behavior while accounting for the unique operational and economic requirements of electricity markets.

  In this talk, I will present online learning algorithms that enable market operators to learn equilibrium prices by sequentially observing suppliers' responses to posted prices. Unlike traditional primal-dual approaches, our algorithms achieve sublinear regret simultaneously across three critical objectives: minimizing unmet demand, production costs, and payments to suppliers. We establish these guarantees under time-varying demand and, with additional contextual information, time-varying supplier costs. Numerical experiments motivated by electricity markets illustrate the effectiveness of our approach, highlighting how learning-based pricing can support reliable and efficient market operations under incomplete information.

bio: |-
  Devansh Jalota is a Jerry and Harriet Thuesen Early Career Professor and Assistant Professor in the H. Milton Stewart School of Industrial and Systems Engineering at Georgia Tech. His research blends ideas from operations research, economics, and computer science to develop datadriven algorithms and incentive schemes for socially responsible market design, with a particular focus on applications in future mobility systems and electricity markets. His research has been featured in The New York Times and recognized through honors including the Stanford Interdisciplinary Graduate Fellowship and multiple distinctions at the INFORMS Annual Meeting. Prior to joining Georgia Tech, he was a Postdoctoral Research Scientist at Columbia University’s Data Science Institute. He received his PhD in Computational and Mathematical Engineering from Stanford University and earned a BS in Civil and Environmental Engineering and a BA in Applied Mathematics from the University of California, Berkeley.
